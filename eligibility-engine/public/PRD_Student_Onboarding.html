Problem & Context

What is the problem?

Nursing students in India face a fragmented landscape of 50+ government nursing exams (AIIMS NORCET, UPPSC, RRB, BTSC, UKMSSB, ESIC, state-level recruitments). Each exam has different eligibility rules — age limits, category relaxations, degree requirements, experience mandates, domicile restrictions.

Students waste hours reading notification PDFs and manually checking if they qualify. Many miss exams they were eligible for. Others waste application fees on exams they can't appear for.

Who is affected?

  • Neha (18-21) — B.Sc. 1st/2nd year, wants to know which exams to target early
  • Kavya (20-23) — B.Sc. 3rd/4th year, needs to map exams to her graduation timeline
  • Priya (23-25) — B.Sc. graduate, re-attempting, racing the age limit
  • Rahul (25-28) — GNM, working 10-hour hospital shifts, needs quick answers
  • Deepak (28-30) — Last attempts remaining, every eligible exam matters

What we're building

An onboarding flow that collects a student's profile in under 45 seconds and instantly shows them which exams they're eligible for. The profile feeds into the Eligibility Engine which evaluates each exam's rules against the student's data.

Design principle

Headspace-inspired "3 screens to value": Collect the minimum mandatory data upfront (7 fields across 2 screens), make everything else optional and skippable, and get the student to their results in under 45 seconds.

Progress bar now shows 2/3. A single card holds three dropdowns: highest school exam passed, nursing qualification, and a "Where are you in your nursing journey?" status field.   • All 3 fields mandatory.
  • Fork logic: status = "Passed Out" → FR 3.4. Any year → skip to FR 3.5.

Conditional — Passed Out only
TL;DR — Only shown to graduates. Captures INC recognition, nursing council, and optional post-qualification work experience. Feeds the engine's council and institute checks.
What the student experiences

FR 3.5 — Anything Else?

Step 3/3 — Optional & Skippable
TL;DR — Step 3 of 3. Optional special-category fields (marital status, PwD, ESM, govt employee, J&K domicile) that drive age relaxations for the small % of students they apply to. Designed to be skipped fast — skip button is above the card.
What the student experiences
The progress bar is fully filled. Above the card, a ghost "Skip — show me my jobs" button gives immediate permission to bypass. The card itself is titled "Anything else?" with a subtitle that says most students skip this. One marital-status dropdown and four toggles (PwD 40%+, ex-serviceman, central govt employee, J&K domicile 1980-89) are stacked tightly. Ex-serviceman ON reveals a "months of service" number input. The final CTA — "Show me all jobs" — promises the payoff.
Rules
  • All fields optional: No validation. Skip or submit works from any state.
  • Skip button position: ABOVE the card, not below.
  • Skip copy: "Skip — show me my jobs".
  • ESM + Govt Employee both ON: Valid. Engine uses Math.max(esmRelaxation, govtRelaxation).
  • Toggle OFF clears sub-fields: ESM months cleared when ex-serviceman toggle goes OFF.
  • Back navigation: Goes to FR 3.4 if passed out, else FR 3.3. App remembers the fork.
  • Final CTA copy: "Show me all jobs".

FR 4.5 — Sort Order

Core Logic
TL;DR — Every student sees every job — sort determines position, not visibility. Deadline is the primary sort (missing a deadline is irreversible). Tie-breakers cascade: vacancies → salary → lifecycle → geography → alphabetical. Tracked jobs get no boost.
What the student experiences
The student never has to hunt for the most urgent jobs — they're always on top. By default, whatever is closing soonest is first; after that, bigger drives (more vacancies, higher pay) naturally surface above smaller ones. Tracking an exam doesn't move it up — tracking is about notifications, not ordering.
Rules
  • Golden rule: Every student sees every job. Sort never hides jobs.
  • Cascade: First rule that differentiates two jobs wins their order.
  • Tracked jobs: Same sort as everyone else. No pinning, no boost. Use the "Tracked" pill to see only them.
  • Custom sort (FR 4.4B): When a student picks a non-default sort (e.g., Highest salary), that rule moves to priority 1 and the rest of the cascade follows.
  • Missing data: Jobs without the primary sort field (e.g., no salary) sink to the bottom within that sort.

When does "Eligible" (green) fire?

"Eligible" fires only when none of the 18 rules above match — i.e., all of these are true:

  • Age is within the exam's min/max (no need to stack relaxations).
  • Degree is in the exam's accepted list.
  • Gender matches any restriction.
  • Academic baseline meets the requirement (e.g., 12th Science if required).
  • If the exam requires Passed Out: student is Passed Out.
  • If the exam requires INC recognition: student's INC field is filled (Yes).
  • If the exam requires nursing council registration: student's council state matches the exam's accepted scope.
  • If the exam requires work experience: student has filled experience months and meets the minimum.
  • If the exam has a marital restriction: student's marital status is filled and matches.
  • If the exam requires a specific language: student has filled languages and they match.
  • Admin has entered all required exam fields for the engine to evaluate.

Important rules

  • First match wins: Checks run in the order above. The first rule that fires determines the tag.
  • Age check — how it works: The engine computes a known max = base max (from admin) + category relaxation + any Step 3 relaxation bonuses the student has already filled in their profile.
    • If studentAge ≤ knownMax → age check passes.
    • If studentAge > knownMax but one or more relaxation inputs are unfilled (PwD, Central Govt, or ESM + service years) → tag as Likely Eligible and prompt for the missing inputs via FR 5.2.
    • If studentAge > knownMax AND every relaxation input has been filled → tag as Verify with Notification.

  • Never block: No eligibility tag prevents a student from tapping the card, reading the detail, or applying. The tag is guidance, not a gate.
  • FR 5.2 coupling: Every "Yes" in the "Prompt on tap" column triggers the contextual data-collection flow from FR 5.2 when the student taps the card.
  • Likely Eligible is the expected default: For most students early in onboarding, "Likely Eligible" is the honest answer because the engine can't fully confirm without complete profile data. Use the contextual prompts (FR 5.2) to progressively convert "Likely Eligible" to "Eligible" as students fill more fields.

FR 4.6.8 — Notification System

Core Logic

When a student tracks an exam, they're signing up for push notifications about every major milestone. Think of it as a personal secretary who taps them on the shoulder at exactly the right moments:

Admit card releasedWhen the admin marks this milestone as done"Your [Exam] admit card is available. Download now."

Result declaredWhen the admin marks the result as out"[Exam] results are out. Check your result now."
Why push notifications only (no in-app inbox): The tracked cards already show an up-to-date timeline with lifecycle badges — that IS the in-app notification surface. Building a separate notification inbox would be redundant. If a student opens the app, they see the timeline. If they don't open the app, the push notification reaches them. Edge case — push notification fails silently: If a push fails, there's no retry or fallback. The student will see the updated timeline and lifecycle badge the next time they open the app.

FR 4.7 — Job Expiry & Past Tab

Core Logic
TL;DR — A job stays in the main feed while any future event exists. Once all events are past AND a state-specific buffer expires, it moves to Past. What the student experiences

Rules

FR 4.10.6 — Progressive Loading

Performance

The jobs feed doesn't load everything at once. It loads in layers — fast things first, heavy things later, and only when needed.

Think of it like walking into a library. You see the shelf labels and book spines immediately (that's layer 1). You pick up a book and read the back cover (layer 2). You only open the book and read the full chapters when you're actually interested (layer 3). You'd never ask the librarian to read every book out loud before you can start browsing.

Layer 1: First 10 cards (instant)

The moment the student lands on the jobs feed, the first 10 job cards load. These carry only the lightweight data — exam name, organization, salary, vacancies, eligibility tag, deadline date, lifecycle badge, and tracking count. This is everything the student needs to scan and decide "is this interesting?"

These 10 cards fill the visible screen. The student can immediately start reading, scrolling, and comparing. No waiting.

Layer 2: Remaining cards (background)

While the student is reading the first 10 cards, the remaining jobs load silently in the background. They appear at the bottom of the feed as they arrive — no "Load more" button, no pagination controls, no interruption. The list just grows.

For a typical feed of 20-50 nursing exams, the full list is ready within 2-3 seconds of landing on the page. The student never notices the loading because they were busy reading the first batch.

Layer 3: Job detail (on tap)

The heavy data — the full eligibility breakdown with every rule explained, the fee matrix with category-wise amounts, the complete exam pattern, the syllabus text, the FAQ list, the strategy video, the vacancy breakdown by category — none of this loads until the student actually taps a card.

When they tap, the detail page appears with its own skeleton (pulsing gray shapes matching the page layout). The full data loads, the skeleton fills in, and the student sees everything. This keeps the feed fast and the detail page rich without making either one pay for the other's weight.

What the student experiences

From the student's perspective, the page is never empty and never slow. They land, they see cards, they scroll, they tap, they see details. The layered loading is invisible to them — which is exactly how it should feel.

Edge case — Very slow connection: If the first 10 cards take longer than a third of a second, the skeleton screen appears (see FR 4.10.1). On extremely slow connections (2G), the skeleton might show for 3-5 seconds.
Edge case — Student scrolls faster than loading: If the student scrolls past the first 10 cards before the background batch arrives, they see a small inline loader at the bottom of the list — just a subtle pulsing dot, not a full skeleton screen.

Logic

If the student taps "Skip for now," the detail page opens with whatever data we have. The eligibility tag might show "Likely Eligible" or "Verify with Notification" — but that's their choice. We never block them from seeing the job.

When the engine stops asking

3-skip ruleIf the student skips a specific field 3 times (across any exam, not per exam), stop asking for that field permanently. The student can still update it manually from the Eligibility Profile.

FR 5.7.1 — Info Tab

Content

The Info tab is where the student finds out exactly how they fit into this exam. Every section is personalised first — telling the student their specific situation — with a "View All" link to see the full breakdown for all categories.

Age Limits

Shows the age limit that applies specifically to this student, based on their category from onboarding.

  • "Age limit for you: 18 years" — personalised, prominent.
  • "View All" link — opens a bottom sheet with category-wise age limits (UR, OBC, SC/ST, PwD, Ex-serviceman, etc.).

Total Seats

Shows seats available in the student's category — not just the overall total.

  • "Seats for your category: 643" — personalised. An OBC student sees the OBC count, a PwD student sees PwD seats, a General student sees UR seats.
  • "View All" link — opens a bottom sheet with category-wise seat breakdown (UR, OBC, SC, ST, EWS, PwD, etc.) with a total at the bottom.
How personalisation works: The student's category (from onboarding Step 1) is matched against the admin-entered vacancy breakdown. If the student hasn't set their category, the count defaults to the total number.

Essential Qualification

A readable breakdown of what degrees and pathways are accepted. For exams that accept multiple routes (e.g., "B.Sc. Nursing OR GNM with 2 years experience"), each pathway is clearly separated with an "OR" label between them.

This section isn't personalised — it's a factual list of all accepted qualifications. The student reads it and identifies their own path.

Edge case — admin hasn't entered category-wise breakdown: "Total Seats" shows the overall number only, no personalised slice. The "View All" link doesn't appear.

FR 5.7.2 — Pattern Tab

Content

Shows the complete exam pattern — how the exam is structured, how many stages it has, and what each stage looks like.

For two-stage exams (like NORCET), each stage gets its own card:

  • Stage name: "Prelims" or "Mains" as a pill label
  • Duration: How long the exam lasts (e.g., "90 Minutes")
  • Format: Number of questions and total marks (e.g., "100 MCQs | 100 Marks")
  • Negative marking: Whether wrong answers cost marks (e.g., "1/3 per wrong answer")
  • Section rules: Whether sections are timed separately, whether back-navigation is allowed
  • Purpose: Whether this stage is qualifying only or merit-deciding
Why "Purpose" matters: Students preparing for NORCET need to know that Prelims is just qualifying (pass/fail) while Mains decides their rank. This changes their study strategy — they might focus differently on each stage. Most notification PDFs bury this in fine print.
Edge case — Single-stage exam: Only one card appears. No "Prelims/Mains" label — just the exam details directly.

FR 5.7.3 — Syllabus Tab

Content

Shows what topics the exam covers, broken into categories.

At the top, a visual bar shows the weightage split — for example, "Nursing Syllabus 70%" and "Non-nursing Aptitude 30%." This immediately tells the student where to focus their preparation time.

Below the bar, each category lists its subjects as a numbered list:

  • Nursing Syllabus: Nursing Foundation, Medical Surgical Nursing, Paediatric Nursing, Anatomy, Physiology, etc.
  • Non-nursing Syllabus: Mental Health Nursing, Nursing Management, Research and Statistics, etc.
Edge case — No syllabus entered by admin: Tab is hidden entirely. The other three tabs redistribute to fill the space.

FR 5.7.4 — Fees Tab

Content

Shows how much the exam costs to apply for — personalised to the student.

At the top, a highlighted box shows:

"Fees for you: Rs. 2,400/-" — computed from the student's category. An OBC student sees the OBC fee. A PwD student sees "Exempted." A general student sees the general fee.

Below the personalised box, a full table shows all category-wise fees for reference — so the student can also see what other categories pay. This is useful for students who are unsure about their category or want to compare.

Why personalise at the top: The student's first question is "how much do I pay?" Not "how much does everyone pay?" Leading with their personal fee answers that question in one glance. The full table is there for reference but it's secondary.
Where the personalisation comes from: The student's category (from onboarding Step 1) is matched against the admin-entered fee table. If the student hasn't set their category, the fee defaults to the General/UR amount.
Edge case — Student is PwD: The personalised box shows "Exempted". If this specific exam doesn't exempt PwD, it shows the applicable reduced amount.
Edge case — No fees entered by admin: Tab is hidden entirely.

FR 5.10.2 — Track / Untrack Button

Button

Tapping either one takes immediate action — no confirmation dialog. Untrack shows a warm toast so the student knows what happened.
